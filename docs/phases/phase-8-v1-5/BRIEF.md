# Phase 8 brief: v1.5

Status: **plan written, waiting for owner approval. Nothing is built.**

## Goal

Make GraphLens better in three ways the owner chose on 2026-10-29: (A) faster and better answers,
(B) real sign-in, (C) a bigger corpus that can grow by itself. Hosting, HTTPS and monitoring are
**not** in this phase (they stay in [LIMITS.md](../../LIMITS.md)). The blueprint text for v1.5 is not
in the repository, so this scope comes from the owner's choice plus the open items in the Phase 7 notes.

## Order and why

Each part ends with its own measurement, owner gate and (for B and C) its own spend or security
decision. I recommend this order because it goes from cheapest and most measurable to costliest.

### Part A: faster and better answers (target: close the 3 missed Phase 7 targets)

Missed: correctness 70% (target 75%), gap +13.3 (target +15), p95 10.3 s (target 8 s).

| Step | Idea | Why it might help | Cost to test (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Streaming of the answer text | The first words appear in about 1 s instead of 5 s; does not change the total time | free (no new model calls) |
| A2 | Shorter answer limit (about 150 words instead of 250) | The model call is most of the 5 s; fewer words, less time | about $0.12 to $0.14 for the hard set (one run) |
| A3 | Better choice of passages inside the 8,000-token context (a reranker or a score that favours gold-like passages) | Only about 57% of the right passages fit today (D68); choosing better might lift correctness | reranker model download (size to be shown first); test about $0.15 |
| A4 | Retry also on relate path search and plain-RAG retrieval; declare `uvicorn` in the pipeline lock | Closes two known reliability gaps | free |

Rule: every variant gets its own system name, is judged on the same 30 hard questions, and is compared
question by question with Phase 7. A change is kept only if it helps and does not hurt the easy set or
the refusal set. If nothing reaches the targets, they stay reported as missed.

### Part B: sign-in (D11)

Replace anonymous visitors with accounts, keeping the anonymous mode working.

- Needs a **decision (D70)** on the method. Options: (1) email and password, hashed with argon2id,
  no email is sent, no password reset in v1.5 (simplest; local use); (2) email magic link (needs an
  email-sending service); (3) a login with Google/GitHub (needs registering an app). Recommended: option 1.
- Existing anonymous data can be claimed by the first account that signs in from the same browser (opt-in, one time).
- Security work is part of the phase, not an extra: argon2id, login rate limits per address and per
  account, lock-out delay, generic error messages (no "this email exists"), session cookie rules as in
  Phase 6, delete-account, tests for access control (a user never sees another user's rows), an updated
  audit and threat note. No claim of "secure" without an outside review.
- Schema: the `users` table already exists in Postgres; a migration adds what is missing.

### Part C: bigger corpus and incremental updates

- Grow from 25 papers to a target the owner picks, and add new papers without re-processing old ones
  (the extraction cache is keyed by content hash, so unchanged passages cost nothing).
- **Cost, linear estimate:** extraction cost about $2.40 for 25 papers (about $0.10 per paper), so
  **100 papers about $7 to $8 more, 250 papers about $22 to $25 more**, plus a few dollars for
  community reports and an evaluation re-run. This is an estimate, not a quote. Each step has `--dry-run`
  and `--max-cost-usd`, and I wait for your yes.
- **Licence check** for every new paper (open licence only) and a manifest with hashes, as in Phase 1.
- **pypdf upgrade (D59):** do it at the start of Part C, because re-ingesting the 25 old papers
  (about $2.40 of re-extraction) is the price of fixing two known advisories before the corpus grows.
  Owner decides (D71): upgrade now, or keep accepting the risk with a fixed corpus.
- **Incremental command:** `add-papers` (download, ingest, embed, extract only new units, resolve
  against the existing graph, update communities). Hard part: community detection on a changed graph.
  First version rebuilds the communities and their reports (cost shown first); a real incremental
  community update is out of scope.
- Re-measure entity resolution and relation precision on a sample of the new part, and re-run the final
  evaluation. A bigger corpus will change every number; the README table is regenerated by script.

## Out of scope

HTTPS, hosting, monitoring, scheduled backups, PDF upload by users, relate/global replay, a second
judge model, multi-language corpora.

## Build steps (after approval)

1. A1 to A4, measure, report (stop for owner gate: keep or drop each change).
2. Part B: design note and threat list, migration, API, web screens, tests, review.
3. Part C: pypdf decision, manifest of new papers, dry-run estimate, ingest and extract in batches, rebuild
   graph and communities, quality sample, final evaluation.
4. Release notes, README table regenerated, tags, A to J report, owner approval.

## Risks

- Part A may not reach the targets; then they stay reported as missed.
- Sign-in adds real attack surface (password guessing, account enumeration, session theft). Mitigations are listed above; no outside review.
- A bigger corpus can lower accuracy (more look-alike entities) and raise cost and latency.
- Monthly OpenAI spend: this month is already near the limit; Part C needs the limit raised by the owner, which I never do myself.
